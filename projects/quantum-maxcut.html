Quantum vs. Classical Max-Cut

A comparative exploration of quantum and classical approaches to the Max-Cut optimization problem, focusing on solution quality and algorithm behavior.

Python Quantum Computing QAOA Optimization

Problem

Max-Cut is a combinatorial optimization problem where the goal is to divide the vertices of a graph into two groups so that the number, or total weight, of edges crossing between the groups is maximized.

This project explores how a quantum optimization approach compares with classical optimization methods on Max-Cut instances.

Approach

I implemented a quantum approach using the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) and compared its behavior against classical methods.

The project focuses on evaluating how the approaches differ in optimization behavior, solution quality, and performance across graph instances.

QAOA

QAOA is a hybrid quantum-classical algorithm designed for combinatorial optimization. A parameterized quantum circuit generates candidate solutions, while a classical optimizer updates the circuit parameters to improve the objective value.

Maximize Cut(G) by assigning graph vertices to two partitions.

Why This Project Matters

This project helped me explore the connection between optimization, graph problems, and emerging quantum algorithms while also comparing them with established classical techniques.

It also gave me experience thinking critically about when a newer computational method offers meaningful advantages and how those advantages should be evaluated experimentally.
